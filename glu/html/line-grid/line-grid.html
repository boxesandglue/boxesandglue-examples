Lines in register across columns

A sheet of paper is never quite opaque. When the lines on the back of a leaf fall between the lines on the front, the grey of the far side shows through and the page looks restless. Printers call lines that meet from both sides in register.

The same care pays off inside a single page. In two columns the eye moves sideways as well as down, and lines that almost, but not quite, stand level look like a mistake.

Where the error comes from

Running text with one line height keeps its rhythm by itself. A heading of another size breaks it, and so does any margin that is not a whole number of lines. The offset then stays to the foot of the column.

Figure 1: a top float, here without a picture.

The column beside it knows nothing of this and starts its own count, which is why the two drift apart.

What the grid does

With line-snap each line finds the nearest line of a grid that belongs to the page. What is left over goes into the margins around the heading, so it keeps its distance to the text below in proportion.
